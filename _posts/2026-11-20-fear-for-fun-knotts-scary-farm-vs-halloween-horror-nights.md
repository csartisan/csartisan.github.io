For thousands of years, Halloween has been one of the most decorative and expressive holidays, allowing people to be as creepy as possible. As the rave for the holiday has gotten more demanding, theme parks have found new ways to turn fear into entertainment. 

Two of California's most popular Halloween attractions, Halloween Horror Nights and Knott's Scary Farm, attract thousands of thrill-seekers every year. But behind the terrifying mazes and constantly chasing characters lie concerns about crowd control, guest behavior, and overall safety. 

One major difference between the two events is ticket pricing. Tickets for Knott's Scary Farm start at $65, while Halloween Horror Nights start at around $95. But to get the bang for your buck, you almost always want to pay more. “When deciding which park to go to, my friends and I naturally wanted the one that cost less to get into,” sophomore Trista Wu said. 

Lower prices make parks like Knotts more accessible to different crowds, and what you get becomes more worth it. 

“There’s less advertising and fewer sponsors at Knotts, so they have the right to make it even scarier, which makes it more fun.” Conservatory teacher Robert Blair said.

However, both parks face large crowds thanks to the Halloween experience’s high demand, so ticket prices don’t determine the guest amount as much as they used to. 

According to the US Universal Hollywood Community, Universal has responded to traffic congestion by making “Early Scare” passes, where they let guests enter the park early at 2 p.m., giving them access to select houses before the nighttime hits. With strategic scare attractions and packed attractions, Halloween should be about enjoying the scare and not putting others at risk.
