When we look up skyward at our boundless universe, it stares back at us with the eyes of thousands of tiny sparkling specks. Every fiber of our own bodies and everything we’ve ever known was constructed by billions of tiny atomic specks that once were ablaze in the cosmos. On Earth, too, we humans have invented something that seems to shine like the stars in the galaxy–glitter! Commonly found decorating faces at a music festival, theme-driven mega-clubs, or toddlers’ birthday parties, glitter is an accessory that, like the universe, seems to have no bounds or limits. Once released, glitter seems to linger and even multiply, persisting not only on all one’s belongings but in the crevices of life one would least expect to find them. Far away from its typical environment of flashing lights and booming music might be the most surprising presence of glitter yet: within the boundaries of our very own school. 

“Of course I want glitter, it makes me really happy,” is what junior Layne Brisbois exclaims, as the second of thirty-one students who welcomed a glitter bomb within a fifty-student sample. “Definitely the recipients had kind of waves of joy hitting them,” is what ceramics teacher Mr. Tomlinson says, after witnessing what he describes as the “ceremonial” sharing of glitter in his classroom and echoing the same delight that was expressed by other glitter fans.

 Vix Smith, a senior, excitedly declared, “So *you're* the glitter person!”, only twenty minutes after the first sparkly victims were claimed. It appears that, in that short amount of time, word had already spread like wildfire–or like glitter. Commenting on the presence of glitter on campus, history teacher Mr. Pagan saw a hidden meaning, saying, “It shows who’s willing to spread joy around the campus.” 

Lying concealed behind a pretty accessory, or what some may consider an itchy mess, is, in fact, something deeper that draws people in. Glitter reminds us that the smallest things can provide big laughs and great joy through the simple act of sharing, just like the stars freely sharing their light and infusing us with a sense of wonder. Perhaps, by inventing glitter and other shiny little things, humans were trying to capture a bit of the universe in an attempt to draw it closer. Or put another way, trying to lasso a little piece of heaven and bring it to us on Earth. The burden of possessing a sliver of heaven, however, is deciding what you choose to do with it.
