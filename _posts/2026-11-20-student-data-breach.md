CSArts students may have had their personal information compromised following a statewide data breach.

The breach occurred within Educational Testing Service, a nonprofit organization that administers state and federally required testing throughout California public schools. According to the California Department of Education, the information may include student names, dates of birth, addresses, demographic information, disability status, participation in certain school programs, and state test results. Social Security numbers, driver’s license or passport numbers, financial account information, credit or debit card information, account passwords, and parent or guardian personal information were not affected.

“When the state notifies you that student data has been breached, [it’s] very frustrating. However, our systems have not been affected in any way. If anything, it just reminds us how important it is to protect student data,” said Mrs. Read.

The Educational Testing Service is providing two years of complimentary identity-protection services through Experian IdentityWorks. Students should remain cautious about opening emails and text messages from unverified senders.

For more information, please visit the CDE’s dedicated webpage: ++[www.cde.ca.gov/ets](http://www.cde.ca.gov/ets)++.
