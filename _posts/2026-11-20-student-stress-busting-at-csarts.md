Stress comes in many different ways, always finding its way into a person's life regardless of what you do, bringing the question: what do you do? 

Stress comes from a nervous system reaction. It can last from a few minutes to feeling like it’s never-ending. It's rooted in outside pressures, daily tasks, and your own thoughts. It can cause things like headaches, fatigue, stomach aches, and trouble sleeping. It can also bring irritability, anxiety, trouble concentrating, and racing thoughts. 

It brings a lot, but community always finds a way.

“I listen to music and take a second to myself. I also love to crochet because it keeps my hands busy,” said Oliver WainWright. 

There is no known number of ways to combat stress; it's simply endless. Stress relief practices are built into healthy daily habits. Anything you find enjoyable can relieve stress.

“When I'm stressed, I love to bake cause it lets me work on something else,” said teacher Katherine Winans. 

Not moving can make stress and anxiety worse. Stress begins to linger in the body longer than it should. Science shows that physical movement, breathwork, and consistent mindfulness can all help manage stress. Simply moving your hands begins to quiet the brain and its worries, lowering stress levels after 15-20 minutes of continuous action. 

Our school has its own resource to share as well.   “The Wellness Center was created to provide a safe, supportive space where students can take a break, recharge, and prioritize their mental health and emotional well-being. Our goal is to create an environment where students feel comfortable seeking support, develop healthy coping strategies, and know they are valued and heard,” said Alisha Miranda.  The Wellness Center is located in room 111.
