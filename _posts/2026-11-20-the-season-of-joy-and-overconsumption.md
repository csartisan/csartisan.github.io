What comes to mind when you think of holiday shopping? Unlimited deals, shopping sprees, last-minute presents to buy? Easter, Hanukkah, and Christmas are all examples of holidays that people from all over the world celebrate, presenting opportunities for gift shopping. Holidays represent a time of giving and embracing familial traditions. Although holidays are an important tradition for many families, there are many side effects that arise from overspending during the holidays. According to the Center for Biological Diversity, the amount of household waste in the US is at least 23% higher in the month of December. Christmas is one of the biggest holidays celebrated globally, and through major consumerism, people spend a large portion of their money on paper, ribbons, etc. The waste produced can end up in landfills and can get transferred into natural ecosystems. Additionally, the amount of energy used is significantly higher during the holidays, with Christmas lights taking up around 66 billion kWh every year according to the US Department of Energy. Many decorations require electricity, such as inflatables, and are a major contributor to energy usage.  

Overconsumption can also lead to increased carbon emissions. In fact, transportation and passage of cargo tend to be the highest during the holidays. One of the reasons for overconsumption can be social pressure. 

“I think there’s a bit of status with spending, with the more there is to fill, the more you feel like you made it. It puts people in a situation where they can’t match the energies of the people around them,” assistant principal Sean Kerr said. The increase in pressure is also linked to social media, which has a tremendous effect on how the holidays are advertised. 

Akshaya Venkata, an 11th grader in Visual Arts, feels very strongly about social media’s impact on consumer consumption. “I think social media creates a need for families to do different themes at Christmas; for example, last year it was like a Ralph Lauren Christmas, and everyone started buying up bears and decorating their whole trees with the Ralph Lauren colors.” Although many influencers often advertise a variety of brands, claiming these brands will make people’s lives better, homemade gifts can show more care and thought. A gift that took two hours to make compared to one bought in a minute makes a difference. 

The holidays are a time of celebration and joy for families. It’s a special occasion to appreciate what’s around you and be grateful for what you have. Even if consumption plays a huge role in the holidays, we can take the time to reflect on what the holidays actually mean and be more mindful of the effects of over-consumption. Gifts and decorations are a fun part of the holidays, but spending time with family and friends is the heart of it.
