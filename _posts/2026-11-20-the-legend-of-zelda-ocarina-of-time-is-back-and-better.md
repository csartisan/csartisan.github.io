*The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time* is arguably considered the most groundbreaking video game in history. Being the first-ever 3D Zelda game alone was a feat, but along with that came with snappy new gameplay, a magnificent music score, a dark and compelling story, and a jarringly huge world to explore. From that point on, in a way, most of the games in the series succeeding it were along the lines of “what if it was *Ocarina of Time,* but…” For example, what if it was *“Ocarina of Time* but more gritty and now Link’s a wolf?” Or, “what if it was *Ocarina of Time* and the Hyrule was flooded? What if it was *Ocarina of Time* but they all ride birds instead of horses and live in the sky?”  

I’m getting carried away here, but you get the point. This game was a massive push for the series and the world of gaming overall. The question is: can such a record-breaking game be perfectly recreated?  


While the new *Ocarina of Time* remake was released last week, the fanbase has been strictly divided on opinions for how the game would turn out. The main thing you might notice is that the graphics of the remake are much better than the original game. This significantly changes the visuals. The original game was made up of a bunch of sharp-edged shapes, while now the remake’s world looks almost identical to real-life Earth.

Now here’s where opinions split: will the realism ruin the feeling from the original?

If anything the fans can agree on, it’s the fact that the world looks absolutely stunning. The critiques mainly center around the character designs. They have a vast amount of detail stitched into the clothing, faces, and items. Some fans argue that it’s over-cluttered, and the extensive detail is almost nauseating to look at. Others say that the detail is actually perfect for this reason: it does exactly what the original game did. It stretched what games can do to its absolute limit, and now the remake is pushing its absolute visual limits with the Switch 2’s hardware. And on top of that, debatably, the game takes everything and just makes it better: the music, the world, the atmosphere, and the gameplay. Everything about it is completely reimagined for the better, yet somehow still holding onto the essence of the original title.

As we got closer to the game’s release, people gradually settled on the jarring change and have realized that it’s just an advancement of visual storytelling, and most of the judgments are just bad criticism.
