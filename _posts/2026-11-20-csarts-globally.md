Spending your summer in another country and getting college credit is a dream for many CSArts students. Many high school students don’t realize that they have the opportunity to study abroad, but a growing number have chosen to spend their summers on study programs. 

The CSArts Study Abroad Club gives students the resources to study abroad with the Council on International Educational Exchange (CIEE). The club meets in Mr. Reyes’ room, 404, the first Thursday of the month.

Organizations like CIEE offer a number of study abroad locations and topics, with high school study abroad programs available in six of the seven continents. CSArts students studied in a variety of locations last summer, including France, Spain, Japan, and Morocco. Many programs also offer college credit for students abroad, especially for those studying academic subjects.

CSArts student Jane Kistner (CW ‘28) described her experience with Oxbridge Study Abroad in Cambridge: “I had a really cool teacher; she was an acclaimed poet. I met a lot of people.” 

While many study-abroad programs focus on topics like language or international development, programs are also available in most of the same arts offered in CSArts conservatory classes, from dance to visual arts and design.

Study-abroad programs are unique because they occur in a very different environment from regular high school classes. During summer programs, most of your classes will be with the same limited group of students, and at many programs, students will live in the same neighborhoods and same buildings. 

Dedicated downtime gives students time to connect with their host families, local residents, and their classmates. Students also have much more independence. They manage their own finances, home schedules, and commutes. These factors mean many study abroad alumni describe the programs as  unique compared to other high school academics. CSArts students are used to learning in an atypical academic environment, but study abroad presents new opportunities and challenges for high schoolers– despite these challenges, many CSArts study abroad alumni encourage anyone interested in learning more about the world to apply.
