On November 8th, CSArts held the Fall Festival, an annual event that celebrates the arrival of fall and showcases CSArts’ talented students. It’s an exciting time to hang out with friends and loved ones and enjoy a variety of activities. 

There was something for everyone, from games like Connect Four and bean bag toss to activities for Kindergarteners and elementary school kids. On the school’s sports field, there were huge bounce houses, and if you came with an appetite, food vendors were lined up near the gym. 

Live music performed by CSArts students was the soundtrack for the day. For shoppers, the 200 corridor was lined with Art Marketeers, selling original pieces, and there were also more games. 

In the 100s, students held interactive stations that taught people how to make origami and to dance. These stations weren’t just about the festival, they also allowed students to interact and highlighted the many different art forms CSArts offers. Students were able to represent their conservatory and showcase what their conservatory could be like. A junior who participated in the Origami and Storytelling station shared her experience, reflecting on the value of the station. Mariam Gharazaryan, a Creative Writing student, said, “The Origami Activity was a beautiful way to learn to do something and have it be a success or be a failure, but then turning that origami into inspiration. It reflects Creative Writing in how inspiration really can come from anywhere and perfection isn’t always necessary to make something amazing.”

When it came to the Visual Arts conservatory, the Art Marketeers had their first kickoff at the Fall Festival. The Art Marketeers is a class where students learn how to sell their art and fairly price it for sale. Elise Barrios, a junior in Art Marketeers explains, “The Art Marketeers are a group of visual art students who are creating their own brands and using that art to find opportunities to sell it.” Through Art Marketeers, students got to set up their own tables and invite people to buy their art. Common items distributed throughout the tables included stickers and prints. One of the tables even had a free DIY print-making station, and unique accessories were spread throughout the tables.

Many students from the Integrated Arts conservatory used the festival as a way to practice their makeup skills, offering free face painting to visitors. It could be as simple as a design on someone’s cheek, or an animal print on their face. 

All in all, the Fall Festival provided many opportunities for students to learn how to lead different stations, connect with each other and families, and participate in one of the school’s biggest events.
