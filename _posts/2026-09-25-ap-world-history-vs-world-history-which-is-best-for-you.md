Diet or Regular?  Leaded or Unleaded?  AP or CP? So many questions! So many choices! And for World History students, choosing the right class can make all the difference.

AP World History is known for its challenging approach to learning about the same subject. Students choose AP World History for different reasons. For some students, the challenge and academic results of the class are major factors. “I joined AP World History because I wanted a challenging class that would look good on my record, and I also love history,” said current AP World History student, Leia VanAckeren.

For students who enjoy or need more of a challenge, the additional depth of AP World History can also make the material more engaging for students. “If I could switch to regular history, I wouldn’t want to. I think AP World History goes much more in-depth, and it makes concepts more interesting,” VanAckeren said. The AP World History class, however, can require students to manage their time carefully. 

Delight Kim, a junior who has completed AP World History, says, “You have to manage time really well, especially in MR. Chi’s class because it’s easy to get into the flow of the work, but if you’re procrastinating, it gets difficult.” 

One area that students have different opinions about is mastery checks. Some students find them stressful, while others see them as useful preparation for tests. Another AP World Student, Emma Tso said she thinks mastery checks are helpful. “They aren’t really necessary for my learning style, but I think they’re fine. They don’t stress me out much, but I feel like we are properly prepared for them.” 

Leia VanAckeren also felt that mastery checks helped her prepare for tests, saying, “I feel very prepared for the tests. The mastery checks are very similar to the tests; with history you just have to know the material.” 

Students in CP World History can face challenges as well. Angelina Grimaldi, a junior who finished regular world history, said, “Preparation for tests would sometimes be difficult because of the limited time between receiving a study guide and taking the test. We would have a day or two to study.” Another factor to consider is: learning style. Some students enjoy detailed material to go more in depth into and challenge themselves academically. Others may find that regular World History better matches their preferred pace and study habits. 

Ultimately, World History is a better fit for students who prefer a slower pace and less of a workload. Considering your own interests, study habits, time-management skills, and your overall mindset as a student can help you decide which course is right for you.
