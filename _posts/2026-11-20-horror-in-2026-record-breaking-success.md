2026 has been a great year for horror fans. Projects from new filmmakers and established directors have brought a huge variety of high-quality horror to the screen.

On May 15, *Obsession,* directed by Gen Z filmmaker Curry Barker, came to theaters. It made over $400 million at the box office, and left a lasting impact on horror for the rest of the year. The film, a feature-length adaptation of a short film Barker published on YouTube, follows shop clerk Bear as he reckons with the dangerous consequences of making a supernatural wish that causes his friend Nikki to fall in love with him. Inspired by classic *Monkey’s Paw* stories, the film was immensely popular with critics and audiences.

Later that month, another horror film from a Gen Z director was released in theaters. Kane Parsons’ *Backrooms* achieved massive commercial success. While not all horror fans resonated with the unique “liminal space” style of horror, relying on heavy atmosphere and the uncanny valley rather than traditional scares, the film was widely well received. The film was based on Parsons’ YouTube series of the same name, which Parsons created at only 16 years old as a student at an arts high school.

Established filmmakers also released major projects in 2026; *Resident Evil,* directed by Zach Cregger, released in September, and Mike Flanagan’s TV adaptation of Stephen King’s 1974 novel *Carrie* released a month later in October. *Resident Evil,* a video game adaptation, is the eighth film in the *Resident Evil* franchise and a huge box office success. The film, like the games, centers on a zombie outbreak in the Midwestern city of Raccoon City.

Mike Flanagan’s *Carrie* is the fifth adaptation of King’s classic horror novel, following the struggles of Carrie White, a teen girl entering high school for the first time and discovering her telekinetic powers. Flanagan is a prolific creator in the horror genre, known for *The Haunting of Hill House* and the previous King adaptation, *Dr. Sleep.* Flanagan, known for his emotionally grounded approach to the horror genre, centered *Carrie* on the experiences of contemporary American high schoolers with issues like cyberbullying and school violence. The series is the first in an upcoming franchise based on King’s work.

2026 has been a historic year for the horror genre, with the emergence of new styles and new directors, record-breaking box office successes, and the beginning of new franchises. More is sure to come in the next few months, finishing a year that has already brought much to fans of the genre.
