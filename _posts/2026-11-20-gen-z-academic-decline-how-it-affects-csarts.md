“They’re the first generation in modern history to score lower on standardized academic tests than the one before it,” says Dr. Jared Cooney Horvath to the New York Post on the reported academic decline throughout the country. In spite of this, however, CSArts has consistently been climbing to new heights academically, year by year.

As Gen Z matures into adulthood, a worrying statistic has appeared. According to standardized tests, Gen Z is doing worse academically compared to previous generations. 

At CSArts specifically, this can take form through students’ refusal to try when they feel like they may be wrong. “Making an educated guess has become more taboo,” says Adam Williams, a teacher for juniors in AP Lang, “and students even in advanced level classes often find themselves opting out of a difficult question or one that they can't answer the whole question.” Williams also says that students seem to be reading less outside of class in recent years, which could affect their overall attitude towards reading and writing.

One cause of decline could be COVID-19. “I think a lot of it is that in recent years, we’re getting closer and closer to students who experienced distance learning in COVID,” says Chak Woo, an Algebra II teacher. “The kids I have now–sophomores and juniors, six years ago, they were in elementary school. They’re missing those formative years where they learn most of those important skills.” Woo says that foundational skills, both numerically and literacy-wise, are necessary for math, especially with word problems. 

Beyond building foundational skills, the learning environment of the lockdown was not ideal for students’ development. “My belief is that education shouldn't always be comfortable because challenge breeds success and grit,” says Williams. He believes that due to supportive home environments during COVID, students may not have been placed in challenging situations that would force them to learn and grow. 

Regardless of these drawbacks, CSArts has been hitting it out of the park, both with increasing state testing scores and honor roll distinctions according to the CSArts 2025-2026 Academic Profile. 

There are many factors that could cause this, but one that sticks out the most is that CSArts is home to many veteran teachers. “My old school had a lot of turnover, and we found that students' gaps were getting bigger because you couldn't really predict what teachers the year before had taught them if they weren't around anymore,” says Williams. He finds that CSArts’s community of veteran teachers helps teachers to deliver content more effectively. 

There is, however, a noticeable gap between English and STEM (Math and Science) scores in standardized testing. The difference between English and Math specifically, Woo says, can be explained by the fact that students are not able to take English classes over the summer. “Students are able to take math classes over the summer to change their level,” says Woo. He says that there is a possibility that lower state testing scores in math could be related to this split. 

Even so, CSArts students still continue to shine brightly in math classes. “I think the attitude is very much the leading factor in their success,” says Woo. He finds that students who consistently take action and show effort not only succeed more, but also push him to provide more support to help them to achieve that goal.
