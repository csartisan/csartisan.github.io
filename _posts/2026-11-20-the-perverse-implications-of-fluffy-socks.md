Sigmund Freud once famously said, “Sometimes a cigar is just a cigar.” The flip side of that statement, however, is that often a cigar is more than a cigar. The same thing can be said about fashion. 

Often an item of clothing is not just an item of clothing. It is a symptom of something else. There is a secret dark side to fluffy footwear. Fluffy socks are designed for comfort and to be worn at home. It’s likely that one reaches for them freshly out of the shower, or before bed in wintertime, but these days more than ever fluffy socks sit beneath desks at our schools.

 In a poll conducted at CSArts, 6 out of every 10 fluffy sock owners report wearing them to school. Those who reserve this indulgence for home life do so because, for them, fluffy socks are too hot or big to be worn with shoes. There is also the fear that extensive use might cause undue wear and tear, thus destroying the thing they love. But some bear these impracticalities for a fraction of extra comfort. This is a mystery that must be investigated. 

According to James, a senior at CSArts, wearing fluffy socks “makes the long day go by faster,” despite the discomfort it may cause. Several others, including Juliet Mandella, related their timeline of increased fluffy sock indulgence to increasing academic stress, although there's no way to tell if they're directly related. This may indicate that fluffy socks are a small way to cope with the increasingly demanding school environment. 

Mr. Pagan recalls fluffy socks as “mainly a winter occurrence,” and Mr. Blair recalls them unpleasantly as being too hot for feet in the summer. Today, however, fluffy socks are just one example of students embracing comfort in situations where it might once seem impractical.     

Other popular but aesthetically problematic clothing among students, such as sweatpants and Ugg boots, demonstrate that modern consumption is partially driven by a grasp for comfort. As schools increasingly emphasize achievement to combat academic competition and the difficult job market, expectations of professionalism and presentability may be sacrificed for comfort. If even our socks are becoming tools to survive the school day, what does that say about our environment? Is comfort just a trend or a method to cope with a productivity- obsessed system that expects us to work like machines? People smoke cigars for comfort too, despite their injurious health consequences. Cigars can also represent not only a change of mood but also wealth and status–not to mention other things not fit for the reading of student eyes. Similarly, fluffy socks are worn by people on all occasions, not just at home, despite their obvious discomfort and excessive heat problems, because they represent a kind of comfort and stress relief they need from work and school. Sometimes, then, a fluffy sock is just a fluffy sock. Often, though, it’s much more than that.
