It’s what feels like a century-long debate, is *The Nightmare Before Christmas* a Halloween or Christmas movie? And while you may be quick to say it’s a Christmas movie due to when it’s set, or it’s a Halloween movie due to the protagonist, Jack Skellington, embodying the spirit of Halloween, I think the answer is much more esoteric than either of these. But before we get into that, we have to first discuss why *The Nightmare Before Christmas* is neither a Halloween or Christmas movie.

First of all, to say it’s a Christmas movie due to the fact that it’s set during Christmas is a weak argument. Would you also say Die Hard is a Christmas movie? Many joke that it is, but we all know it’s an action thriller that just so happens to be set during Christmas. The movie does not elicit any holiday joy, and if you were to change the season it was set in, all we would get are a few changes in dialogue. For something to be a Christmas movie, the entire movie has to have, at its heart, that Christmas zeal. The same goes for any Halloween movie.

So where does The Nightmare Before Christmas land? I find that because it describes the emotions elicited by multiple holidays, *The Nightmare Before Christmas* is simply a movie about Holidays. And the wonder and escape it brings us. So to title it a Christmas or Halloween movie is doing a disservice to the movie as a whole. It’s more than that; it’s a Holiday movie and can be watched any time of the year.
